BPM counts tick, tock and both blocked intervals together as one full swing. The 300-swing mean averages accepted full-swing durations, each calibrated using the established PPS dual EWMA. Positive differences mean the tick interval is longer; negative means the tock interval is longer. Open-time imbalance is 100 × (tick − tock) / full beat. Block-duration mismatch is 100 × (tick_block − tock_block) / mean block duration. Both use the averaged durations.

Use open-time imbalance as a centring guide: zero means equal open times. Block mismatch separately shows unequal beam-crossing durations. These are timing diagnostics, not a calibrated sensor offset or proof of correct centring; motion asymmetry and beam/flag geometry can also affect them. The sign does not identify a physical left or right adjustment.

Estimates & assumptions

Velocity is the speed magnitude at the sensor, estimated as 3.79 mm divided by each blocked duration. Angular semi-amplitude is velocity × measured full period / (2π × 1,242 mm), converted to degrees. This assumes approximately harmonic motion and a sensing region near bottom dead centre. Amplitude is centre to peak; peak-to-peak is twice this angle. The optical switching distance is provisional, so absolute velocity and amplitude are uncalibrated. Impulse acceleration can mix incoming and outgoing motion. Tick/tock does not identify physical left/right travel.

The cycle uses medians from up to 20 complete 15-swing cycles, retaining separate directions. Phase zero follows the swing sequence; it is not a measured impulse time. A dashed marker identifies the largest positive median speed increase between like-direction passages, when at least three complete cycles are available. It is a candidate impulse-related feature, not a force or contact measurement. The phase axis is nominal cycle time, approximately one second per half phase. Gaps and calibration interruptions restart the motion window.

Environmental means use fresh history observations over the measured duration of the corresponding 300-swing mean. A window requires coverage to within 30 seconds of its start and no sampling gap exceeding 30 seconds. Learning observations can supply that environmental history; period must have completed learning. All fits use the same complete paired bucket averages with equal weight. Empty buckets stay empty; measurement boundaries remain separate.

Approximate 95% intervals use Newey–West to account for autocorrelation and unequal error variance. They require consecutive time buckets, at least 30 averages and five dependence windows. The dependence window spans at least the longest measured swing window in that segment. Intervals assume a stable linear relationship and dependence fading within that window; slower drift and shared calibration may add dependence.

Predictors are centred and scaled before fitting. Adjusted slopes are withheld when the scaled predictor condition number exceeds 30 or the model is rank deficient. That threshold is a display policy; correlated variables can prevent their separate contributions being identified even when the fitted period follows the observations closely.

R² and RMS describe the fitted history. Adding predictors can improve these values without improving prediction elsewhere. Association does not establish cause; no response delay or environmental compensation is applied. Refreshes every 30 seconds in a live range.
